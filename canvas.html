Act 1 — Getting Started
Seven screens: first launch through returning user.
Job to be done: Get from zero to a running Amplifier session inside Canvas with the minimum possible friction.

You’ve heard about Canvas. Maybe you’re skeptical — you already use Amplifier from the terminal and it works fine. You download it, open it. The window appears. It’s warm, quiet, mostly empty. There’s nothing to learn because there’s almost nothing on screen. The only question is: do you create a project? The whole act takes under a minute. By the end, Amplifier is running and you haven’t left Canvas.

ACT 1 · STEP 1 — Scene 1.1 Welcome
The beat

You open Canvas for the first time. The window is clean — a narrow sidebar on the left shows “No projects yet” with an “Add project” button at the bottom. The main area is warm off-white with a centered message: “Welcome to Canvas — Your workspace for Amplifier sessions, files, and previews.” One button: “Create your first project →.”

Why this moment matters

Canvas’s personality is established before anything functional happens. Warm, not cold. Inviting, not overwhelming. The sidebar is visible but empty — foreshadowing the structure that fills in later. The single call-to-action means zero decision paralysis.

Done when you can verify
  • Sidebar is empty: “No projects yet” centered in the sidebar area
  • Bottom of sidebar: “Add project” button pinned to bottom — bordered pill, centered text, muted color
  • NO “PROJECTS” header anywhere in the sidebar
  • NO “+” button at top of sidebar — there are no projects yet, so no project row, so no [+]
  • NO session rows, NO history section, NO “HISTORY” label
  • The “Add project” button and “Create your first project →” both open the same modal (Step 2a)
  • Main area: centered vertically and horizontally, not left-aligned or top-aligned
  • Logo mark (two offset squares) centered above the welcome text
  • Title reads “Welcome to Canvas”
  • Subtitle reads “Your workspace for Amplifier sessions, files, and previews.”
  • ONE button: “Create your first project →” — nothing else
  • Nothing else on screen — no settings, no getting-started wizard, no tutorial, no onboarding steps

ACT 1 · STEP 3c — Scene 1.4 (Existing path) Existing Project: Session Launched
The beat

You chose “Improve error handling” from the resume list. The modal closes. The sidebar shows the project with one session. The terminal shows the prior conversation — your last exchange from 2 hours ago — then a “session restored” marker. You’re looking at where you left off, cursor waiting for your next message.

Why this moment matters

Resuming a session must feel like picking up a conversation you stepped away from, not starting a new one. The prior context scrolls in first — you see what you asked, what the assistant found, where it stopped. Then the restore marker draws a line between “then” and “now.” No startup banner, no bundle messages — those are for new sessions. This is a continuation.

Done when you can verify
